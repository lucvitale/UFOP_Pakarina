Dengue in Brazil — A Growing Crisis

Brazil is the country most affected by dengue in the world. In 2024, over 10 million cases were recorded — a historic record — along with nearly 6,000 deaths. The disease spreads through the Aedes aegypti mosquito and thrives in warm, humid conditions, making monitoring and prevention more critical than ever.

10M+ cases in Brazil in 2024
~6,000 deaths recorded in 2024
50% of all Latin American cases
4 serotypes circulating simultaneously

Sources: Brazilian Ministry of Health, PAHO — 2024 data

What is Pakarina?

Pakarina is an interdisciplinary initiative developed at the Federal University of Ouro Preto (UFOP) in partnership with UCASAL and Yncrea. It centralizes fragmented data sources related to dengue into a single, unified web platform — improving coordination between technical teams, researchers, educators, and public health institutions.

Our Mission

Pakarina aims to build a sustainable technological ecosystem that strengthens monitoring capabilities, improves information accessibility, and promotes collaboration between technical and non-technical participants. By combining technology, research, and community engagement, Pakarina seeks to create tools that contribute to public health, environmental awareness, and innovation-driven problem solving.
